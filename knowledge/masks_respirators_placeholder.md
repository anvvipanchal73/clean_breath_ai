## Mask Types and Effectiveness against PM2.5

| Mask Type | PM2.5 Filtration | Notes |
|-----------|-----------------|-------|
| N95 / KN95 / FFP2 | ~95% | Must be well-fitted; check seal |
| P100 / FFP3 | ~99.97% | For very high pollution or occupational use |
| Surgical / Medical mask | ~50% | No tight seal; not recommended for air pollution |
| Cloth / fabric mask | ~20–30% | Insufficient for PM2.5 |

## Key Points
- **Fit matters most.** A poorly-fitted N95 provides less protection than the rated 95%.
- Perform a **seal check** (positive and negative pressure tests) each time.
- Replace N95 when it becomes difficult to breathe through, visibly soiled, or damaged.
- Children: standard adult N95 masks do not fit children; use certified child-size respirators.
- People with COPD or severe asthma: consult a doctor before using tight-fitting respirators.

## When to Wear a Mask Outdoors
- AQI > 100 (Moderate/Orange): consider wearing for prolonged outdoor activity
- AQI > 150: wear N95 for all outdoor time
- AQI > 200: wear N95 at all times outdoors; limit time outside regardless

## Myth-Busting
- **"Any mask is better than nothing."** Only partially true. Surgical masks and cloth masks
  offer very limited protection against PM2.5 particles (<2.5 µm diameter).
- **"N95 is too hard to breathe through for children."** This is a real concern.
  Standard N95 respirators are not approved for children; use appropriately sized
  certified respirators only.

## Medical Disclaimer
Mask guidance here is for general air pollution exposure, not medical treatment.
If you have respiratory symptoms, consult a doctor before relying solely on a mask.
