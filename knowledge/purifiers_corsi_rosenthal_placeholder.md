## Understanding CADR (Clean Air Delivery Rate)

**CADR** measures how quickly a purifier removes pollutants in m³/hour (or CFM).
Higher CADR = cleans more air per hour.

**Recommended CADR for a room:**
> CADR (m³/h) = Room Volume (m³) × 0.75
> This achieves approximately 4 air changes per hour (ACH) for PM2.5.

**Example:**
- Bedroom: 4 m × 4 m × 2.5 m = 40 m³ → Minimum CADR: 40 × 0.75 = **30 m³/h**
- Living room: 6 m × 5 m × 2.5 m = 75 m³ → Minimum CADR: **56 m³/h**

Source: AHAM AC-1-2015 standard.

## Myth: Houseplants Clean Indoor Air

**False.** NASA's 1989 study suggesting plants clean air has been widely
misrepresented. The clean-air delivery rate of a houseplant is approximately
**0.023 m³/h** per plant (Cummings & Waring 2020, Air Quality, Atmosphere & Health).
To match a modest HEPA purifier (200 m³/h CADR), you would need ~8,700 plants.
**Plants do not meaningfully improve indoor air quality in real home conditions.**

Source: Cummings, B.E. & Waring, M.S. (2020). Potted plants do not improve indoor
air quality: a review and analysis of reported VOC removal efficiencies.
*Air Quality, Atmosphere & Health*, 13, 137–146.

## DIY Corsi-Rosenthal Box

A low-cost DIY air filter combining four 20×20-inch MERV-13 furnace filters
and a 20-inch box fan. Lab-measured CADR approximately **300 m³/h**.

**Materials (approximate India costs):**
- 4 × MERV-13 filters (20×20 inch): ~₹250 each = ₹1,000
- 1 × 20-inch box fan: ~₹600–800
- Cardboard and tape: ~₹50
- **Total: ~₹1,650–1,850**

**PM2.5 reduction:** ~55% in a 25 m² room (Bliss et al. 2021).

**Assembly:** Arrange 4 filters in a square, filters facing OUT, fan on top
blowing up. Tape all gaps. Run continuously on medium speed.

Source: Bliss, C. et al. (2021). Portable air purifier performance against
PM2.5 in a medium-sized room. Aerosol Science and Technology (preprint).

## Commercial Purifiers – What to Look For
1. HEPA H13 or H14 filter (true HEPA, not "HEPA-like")
2. CADR rating on the label (not just room size in sq ft)
3. Carbon layer for NO2 and VOC if near traffic
4. No ozone-generating ionizers (ozone is a lung irritant)

## Medical Disclaimer
Air purifiers reduce indoor exposure to particulates but do not treat respiratory
disease. If you have asthma, COPD, or other conditions, consult your doctor.
